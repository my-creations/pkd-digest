# Research: Lifestyle measures to slow PKD progression — water, exercise, nutrition

## Summary

High water intake did not slow kidney growth in the definitive 3-year PREVENT-ADPKD randomized trial despite lowering urine osmolality. Regular exercise and healthy-weight, low-sodium eating are consensus recommendations for blood pressure, cardiovascular and kidney protection, but no trial shows exercise directly slows cyst growth. Ketogenic / calorie-restriction approaches show anti-cyst signals in animals and small short human feasibility trials (KETO-ADPKD, GREASE) with weight loss and possible eGFR/liver-volume signals, but evidence is preliminary, with safety concerns (lipids, kidney stones, muscle loss) and KDIGO 2025 advises against their routine use to slow ADPKD.

## Findings

### 1. WATER — high water intake

1. **Claim:** The best long-term RCT found prescribed high water intake did NOT slow total kidney volume (TKV) growth or lower vasopressin marker copeptin vs ad libitum drinking. **Sources:** PREVENT-ADPKD, Rangan et al., NEJM Evidence (2024). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.
   - Explanation: 184 adults ADPKD Mayo 1B–1E, eGFR ≥30, 3 years. Prescribed water to target 24-h urine osmolality ≤270 mOsm/kg vs ad libitum. Annualized ht-TKV change 6.8%/yr vs 7.8%/yr, mean difference −0.97%/yr (95% CI −2.37 to +0.44, p=0.18, NS). Urine osmolality difference −91 mOsm/kg, but only 52.3% adherent and no copeptin reduction. No excess adverse events. Researcher inference: failure to suppress copeptin may explain lack of efficacy; adherence difficulty is itself a finding.
2. **Claim:** Shorter/uncontrolled studies and a 12-month high-water feasibility study suggested reduced vasopressin/copeptin, pain and small ht-TKV signal, but were not powered for progression. **Sources:** Wang et al. pilot method study; Novartis/DRINK and Australian feasibility work; Kidney360 feasibility of water therapy 2024 (6-mo usual + 12-mo high water in Mayo 1C/1D). **Support:** interpretation — feasibility/surrogate signals only. **Confidence:** low for disease-modifying effect.
3. **Claim:** Consensus still advises adequate hydration / high water intake for general kidney health and to avoid dehydration, especially on tolvaptan, but NOT as a proven disease-modifying prescription to a urine-osmolality target. **Sources:** KDIGO 2025 ADPKD Guideline Ch.4 Key Takeaways; KDOQI US Commentary on KDIGO 2025 (Am J Kidney Dis 2025). **Support:** interpretation of guideline practice points. **Confidence:** high that guideline lists hydration among 4 basic actions (BP control, healthy weight, low salt, high water) while Cochrane Review 2024 (Interventions for preventing ADPKD progression) finds no proven TKV benefit from water.

### 2. EXERCISE — physical activity

4. **Claim:** No RCT shows exercise slows TKV growth or eGFR decline in ADPKD; benefit is indirect via blood pressure, weight, lipids, sleep, mood, physical function and cardiovascular risk, which matter because ADPKD carries high cardiovascular risk. **Sources:** KDIGO 2025; Kidney BEAM PKD substudy, Briggs et al., Clin Kidney J (2025); Diet and Physical Activity in ADPKD review (PMC). **Support:** interpretation; direct evidence only for functional/QoL outcomes. **Confidence:** high for “no direct anti-cyst proof”, medium for cardiometabolic benefit extrapolated from CKD/hypertension literature.
5. **Claim:** A PKD-specific 12-week digital program (Kidney BEAM, n=60 PKD, waitlist-controlled RCT substudy) improved health-related quality of life and physical health measures. **Sources:** Briggs et al., Clin Kidney J (2025) (Kidney BEAM Trial PKD substudy). **Support:** direct evidence for QoL/function, not TKV. **Confidence:** medium (small, short, self-reported + functional endpoints).
6. **Claim:** Small physiology study found reduced exercise capacity and altered NO/ADMA balance in young normotensive ADPKD vs controls, supporting deconditioning/endothelial hypothesis but not an exercise prescription. **Sources:** Am J Kidney Dis cohort study (2014, n=26 ADPKD +30 controls). **Support:** direct evidence (observational). **Confidence:** low-medium.
7. **Claim:** Practical consensus: aim for standard adult activity (e.g. ≥150 min/week moderate aerobic + 2 days resistance, adapted to ability), maintain healthy weight, avoid prolonged dehydration during exercise; clinicians often advise caution with contact/collision sports, heavy Valsalva lifting and high-risk activities if kidneys/liver markedly enlarged, intracranial aneurysm history, or severe hypertension — individualized advice. **Sources:** KDIGO 2025 lifestyle practice points; general CKD exercise guidance; PKD Foundation / clinician reviews. **Support:** researcher inference synthesizing guidelines — not an ADPKD-specific RCT dose. **Confidence:** medium for safety framing; label as consensus extrapolation, not trial-proven.

### 3. NUTRITION — sodium, protein, weight, ketogenic diets

8. **Claim:** Low sodium intake is consensus-recommended; observational/post-hoc data link higher sodium to faster TKV growth and faster eGFR fall, but no dedicated sodium-restriction RCT in ADPKD. **Sources:** HALT-PKD post-hoc analysis, Torres et al. (2017) — each +18 mmol urinary sodium ≈ +0.43%/yr TKV and −0.09 mL/min/1.73m²/yr eGFR; DIPAK observational cohort, Kramers et al., Kidney Int (2020) — salt but not protein associated with faster progression, partly via copeptin. **Support:** direct evidence (observational/post-hoc). **Confidence:** medium for association, low for causal slowing claim.
   - Guidance commonly cited: ≤2–2.4 g sodium/day (≈5–6 g salt/day), consistent with WHO/KDIGO low-salt advice; HALT-PKD instructed ≤2.4 g/day. Label as consensus + extrapolation.
9. **Claim:** No ADPKD-specific protein restriction beyond general CKD advice; high protein intake is discouraged, but low-protein diets are not proven to slow ADPKD and risk malnutrition. DIPAK found protein intake (from 24-h urea) NOT associated with progression. **Sources:** Kramers et al., Kidney Int (2020); KDIGO 2025; Semin Nephrol review Diet and PKD (2023). **Support:** direct evidence for lack of association in one cohort; guideline interpretation. **Confidence:** medium.
10. **Claim:** Overweight/obesity and higher visceral fat associate with faster TKV growth; weight loss is consensus-advised for high-risk/overweight patients, but intentional weight-loss RCTs for TKV are lacking. **Sources:** KDIGO 2025; KDOQI Commentary 2025; GREASE background literature. **Support:** interpretation of observational data. **Confidence:** medium for association, low for reversibility.
11. **Claim:** Ketogenic diets / fasting show strong anti-cyst effects in mouse, rat and feline PKD models via glucose-dependence / Warburg-like metabolism, reduced cyst proliferation and ketosis. **Sources:** Torres et al. preclinical work (cited in KETO-ADPKD rationale); Cell Reports Medicine introduction (2023). **Support:** direct animal evidence. **Confidence:** high for animal signal, low for human translation.
12. **Claim:** KETO-ADPKD (Cukoski et al., Cell Reports Medicine, 2023, n=66 randomized to ketogenic diet vs 3-day monthly water fasting vs control counseling, 3 months) found ketogenic interventions feasible (95% KD, 85% WF rated feasible), induced ketosis, reduced body weight −7.2% and fat mass −20.5% in KD arm, significantly reduced height-adjusted liver volume −4.73% vs +2.04% control (p=0.011 relative), small non-significant kidney-volume dip (−0.55% vs +0.79% control, p=0.285), and eGFR increase (+5.5% creatinine-based, +13.9% cystatin-C-based, p=0.027 vs control) not seen with fasting. Adverse events mostly mild/transient keto-flu; NMR lipids showed increases needing attention. Authors call it exploratory/feasibility, requiring long-term trial. **Sources:** Cukoski et al., Cell Reports Medicine (2023) (NCT04680780); follow-up ERA 2024 abstract on ketosis moderating volume effect. **Support:** direct evidence for short-term feasibility + surrogate signals. **Confidence:** medium for feasibility/weight/liver signal, low for durable kidney protection (short, small, exploratory, eGFR rise may reflect hyperfiltration/hemodynamic or creatinine-muscle effects despite cystatin-C confirmation).
13. **Claim:** GREASE pilot (Testa et al., PharmaNutrition, 2019, n=3, modified Atkins 3 months) reported tolerability/safety and positive satisfaction; GREASE II (Testa et al. design paper PharmaNutrition 2020; ERA abstracts 2023–2025) is an ongoing 12–24-month randomized superiority trial of Modified Atkins Diet in ADPKD — no definitive efficacy results published; early Kidney Week 2024 abstract signals safety/weight insights only. **Sources:** Testa et al. 2019; 2020 design; ASN Kidney Week 2024 abstract; Nutrients review 2024. **Support:** direct evidence for pilot feasibility only. **Confidence:** low for efficacy (pending).
14. **Claim:** KDIGO 2025 explicitly states mTOR inhibitors, metformin, statins, somatostatin analogs, SGLT2 inhibitors, ketogenic interventions and complementary approaches have NOT been proven to slow eGFR decline and should not be used for that purpose outside trials/evidence. **Sources:** KDIGO 2025 ADPKD Guideline Key Takeaways Ch.4. **Support:** direct guideline wording. **Confidence:** high.

## Consensus versus preliminary / disputed

**Consensus (offer in patient digest as “do this / discuss with clinician”):**

- Control blood pressure to target, maintain healthy weight, eat low-salt (≈<2 g sodium/<5 g salt per day unless otherwise advised), stay well hydrated, avoid smoking, limit alcohol/NSAIDs/dehydration; exercise regularly as able; follow general CKD diet (balanced, not high-protein, avoid excess calories/processed foods). Tolvaptan remains only approved targeted therapy for high-risk/rapidly progressive ADPKD with eGFR criteria — requires extra water drinking and sick-day plan.
- High water intake is sensible supportive care, not a proven brake on cyst growth; do not force extreme water loads, especially with hyponatremia risk, diuretics, advanced CKD or heart failure — personalize.

**Preliminary — promising but NOT routine (frame as “research area, talk to trial team”):**

- Ketogenic diet, time-restricted feeding, monthly water fasting, caloric restriction for TKV/eGFR benefit. Feasible short-term in volunteers with weight/liver-volume signals, but unknown durability, kidney-stone, lipid, nutritional, pregnancy, diabetes-medication and CKD-stage risks. Needs long-term RCT (GREASE II and future KD trials).
- High-dose water to urine-osmolality target to suppress vasopressin — tested and negative in PREVENT-ADPKD; further research would need different population/timing or copeptin-guided approach, not current recommendation.
- Specific protein restriction, alkali, phosphate or “PKD superfood” claims — insufficient ADPKD-specific evidence.

**Disputed / caution:**

- Patient enthusiasm for keto (retrospective series, supervised programs) vs clinician concern about LDL rise, stones, muscle loss and eGFR interpretability; post-hoc ketosis-moderates-volume ERA 2024 analysis is hypothesis-generating, not proof.
- Exercise and contact-sport limits: no hard TKV-based ban; advice varies by kidney/liver size, pain/bleeding history, aneurysm, hernia — shared decision-making.

## Missing evidence

- No long-term (>12-mo) powered keto / fasting RCT with TKV slope + eGFR slope + safety (stones, lipids, muscle, pregnancy) — GREASE II pending.
- No exercise-dose RCT with TKV/eGFR as primary; optimal type/intensity and contact-sport risk thresholds undefined.
- No sodium-restriction RCT in ADPKD; target sodium and interaction with tolvaptan/diuretics unclear.
- Protein, hydration targets by CKD stage/age, and applicability to children, pregnancy, GFR<30, transplant donors — largely unstudied.
